## Responses

- **Role, who are you solving for?:** The Curation Defector: a long-time, discerning viewer who sees watching as a deliberate evening activity, not background noise.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To be reliably pointed to a small number of high-quality, unexpected movies and shows they'll actually finish and enjoy.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them:** The recommendation engine treats one viewing as a fixed preference: one action film produces three franchise sequels (UXR-02, BUG-1091), and the algorithm feels designed to keep them scrolling, not to help them choose (UXR-10).
- **External tools the user is forced to use:** A competitor's curated weekly email of hand-picked films (UXR-04) and personal recommendations from friends (UXR-10). The research doesn't name specific third-party apps or sites.
- **The process, the 3–5 manual steps they take:** (1) Ignore the in-app recommendations; (2) get picks from a friend or the competitor's email; (3) search StreamLine for the exact title; (4) save it to My List on whatever device is at hand, where it may not sync to the TV; (5) eventually cancel in favor of the service doing the curating. Steps 3–4 are inferred from bug reports.
- **Core frustration, where the process feels most "broken":** Bringing an outside pick back into StreamLine. Search only matches exact titles (BUG-1080), and saved titles don't sync across devices (BUG-1058), so the viewer does all the discovery work and StreamLine can still lose the result.
- **The evidence, a quote or behavior from the research:** "It became a warehouse. Volume went up, quality of my evenings went down. A competitor emails me two hand-picked films a week and I watch both." (UXR-04, lapsed subscriber)
